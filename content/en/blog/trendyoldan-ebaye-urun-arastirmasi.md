If you sell on Trendyol, you already know something valuable: which products sell in Turkey and at what price. Whether the same product has demand abroad, on eBay for example, is a different question. This post walks through eBay product research step by step: what to look at, which rules matter, and how to start with a small test.

## First, the rule: why "buy on Trendyol, sell on eBay" is risky

A common idea online is to buy a product cheaply on Trendyol, list it higher on eBay and have the parcel sent straight to the buyer. eBay prohibits this. Under eBay's [drop shipping policy](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/selling-policies/drop-shipping-policy?id=4176), drop shipping is allowed only when a **wholesale supplier** fulfils the order. Buying from another retailer or marketplace and having it shipped directly to your buyer is not allowed. Violations can lead to removed listings, lower search placement, a lower seller rating and account suspension.

Branded goods carry a second risk: through eBay's **VeRO** programme, rights owners can have listings they consider unauthorised taken down. If you sell someone else's brand, you need to be able to show you are an authorised seller.

So in this post we treat Trendyol data as a **demand and price signal**, not a supply source. Products you can sell abroad usually come from:

- Products you make yourself (workshop, textile, jewellery, home goods),
- A wholesaler or manufacturer you have a written agreement with,
- Your own catalogue that you already sell on Trendyol.

## 1. Look at sold listings, not active ones

Many active listings don't mean a product sells. The real signal is **sold** listings. After searching on eBay, turn on the "Sold items" filter to see which listings actually sold recently and at what price.

Check:

- **Sales frequency:** steady sales over recent weeks, or a few one-off sales?
- **Sold vs active:** if sold listings are few compared with active ones, competition is high and demand limited.
- **Seller location:** are sales going only to local US sellers, or do sellers shipping from abroad also sell?

## 2. Find the price band

Sort sold prices from lowest to highest and focus on the middle range. Both ends usually mislead: damaged items, bundles or well-known brands. Think about where your product could sit. A parcel from Turkey arrives later than one from a US seller, so your price has to account for that.

Your Trendyol price is a reference point here: is the gap between the Turkish price and the eBay sold price large enough to cover shipping and fees?

## 3. Calculate fees and shipping up front

eBay fees depend on the site your account is registered on and on the category. The structure commonly cited for most ebay.com categories is a 13.6% final value fee on the total (item + shipping) up to $7,500, plus $0.30–$0.40 per order, and an extra 1.65% international fee when the buyer is outside the US ([Taxomate](https://taxomate.com/blog/ebay-seller-fees)). These figures come from a secondary source; check eBay's own fee page for your account.

On shipping:

- **Weight and volume:** international carriers charge by volumetric weight. Light but bulky products (cushions, baskets, large decor) can cost more than expected.
- **Customs:** the US duty exemption for low-value parcels (de minimis) has been suspended since 29 August 2025, so small parcels to the US are dutiable. See our [2026 micro-export guide](/en/blog/mikro-ihracat-rehberi-2026).
- **Payouts:** sellers in Turkey usually receive eBay payouts through an international payment account such as Payoneer ([ikas](https://ikas.com/tr/blog/ebayde-satis-yapmak)). Check the current options in your eBay payout settings.

A simple formula: **eBay sale price − eBay fees − shipping − duty/processing − product cost − packaging = net profit.** If that is close to zero, the product isn't right for you, however many units sell.

## 4. Look at competition and listing quality

Open the first page of sold listings and check:

- Which words repeat in titles? What do buyers call the product?
- How professional are the photos? White background or lifestyle?
- How complete are the item specifics?
- What are the handling times and return terms?

If most competing listings are weak (few photos, missing specifics, short titles), a well-prepared listing has a better chance to stand out.

## 5. Start with a small test

However good the research, sales give the real answer. Start with 5–10 products, watch views and sales for a few weeks, then expand. eBay's [Growth Program](https://export.ebay.com/tr/growth-program) for businesses in Turkey offers onboarding training and an adviser to businesses with more than 100 products to sell; check the conditions on the programme page.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I research products for eBay?

Search for the product, use the "Sold items" filter, and note sales frequency and price range. Then subtract fees, shipping and duty to get net profit, and test with a small group of products.

### Can I buy products on Trendyol and sell them on eBay?

Buying from another marketplace and having it shipped directly to your buyer breaks eBay's drop shipping policy, which allows drop shipping only from wholesale suppliers. Sell products you make or buy from an authorised supplier.

### What fees does eBay charge?

On ebay.com, most categories carry a final value fee of about 13.6% plus $0.30–$0.40 per order, with an extra international fee when the buyer is outside the US. Rates vary by category and by the site your account is registered on.

### How do I receive eBay payouts in Turkey?

Sellers in Turkey usually receive payouts through international payment accounts such as Payoneer. Check which options are available in your eBay payout settings.
